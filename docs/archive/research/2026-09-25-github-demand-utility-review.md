# GitHub Signals for Engineering Decision Skills: Demand and Utility Review

- Research date: 2026-09-25
- Scope: Public GitHub repositories, their own READMEs and skill files, and the local portfolio/evaluation record. Repository descriptions are treated as claims about intended use. Stars, forks, and activity are weak adoption proxies; they do not establish user pain, usage, or impact.

## Executive assessment

### Product synthesis after author clarification

The author considers the existing five tasks useful as a compact set for people close to day-to-day engineering work. The intended product is **assisted analysis and decision support**, not a broad engineering-manager advisor. The five entry points remain: allocate engineering investment, validate a metric and its change, assess an existing proposal, learn from completed work, and communicate established conclusions. This narrows the audience framing and prevents the upstream competitor's broader people/team-management scope from becoming a roadmap for this repository. It also makes the differentiation claim more disciplined: personal writing explains provenance, while the value hypothesis is whether the bounded workflows preserve evidence states and produce usable next decisions. That hypothesis is not yet established by local evals or public GitHub signals.

There are credible signs of **interest in packaging engineering judgment as agent workflows**, but public GitHub evidence does not establish a broad, underserved market for this exact bundle. Matt Pocock's engineering skills repository has substantial visible attention, while Han and newer engineering-management bundles describe adjacent workflows. These are supply-side signals: authors chose to publish and maintain tools. They do not show that managers use them in consequential decisions or that the workflows improve outcomes. ([Matt Pocock skills repository](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills), [Han](https://github.com/testdouble/han), [humantorch/skills-to-pay-the-bills](https://github.com/humantorch/skills-to-pay-the-bills), [bettersense](https://github.com/shwetank/bettersense))

The strongest direct competitor found is [manager-dot-dev/manager-skills](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills): it already offers broad support for engineering managers, including roadmaps, developer productivity, team capacity, business framing, stakeholder communication, and team context, and it is explicitly grounded in its author's writing and experience. Its GitHub page showed 113 stars and 7 forks on the research date. This means “management skills derived from my blog” is not a sufficient distinction. After the author's clarification, the product boundary is five bounded analysis/decision tasks for people close to engineering delivery, not a comprehensive management assistant. Its possible value hypothesis is applying explicit evidence states and bounded conclusions across investment, metrics, proposal review, retrospective learning, and communicating established findings. Whether users value this or get better decisions from it remains untested.

**Evidence of utility is currently limited and local.** The repository records small, evaluator-scored prompt checks, including paired no-skill/skill cases for some workflows; it explicitly notes that these are not blind studies or broad effectiveness evidence. That is a useful quality-control practice, not evidence of user adoption or better organizational decisions. ([Local portfolio review](../methodology/skills-portfolio-review.md), [management-retro evaluation](../methodology/management-retro-evaluation.md))

Recommended wedge: validate `$metric-decision` with a small number of real, de-identified cases where a metric changed near an instrumentation, definition, or cohort change. Compare the skill-guided output with the team's normal analysis, and have decision owners judge whether it surfaced a consequential comparability gap, changed an action, or saved review effort. Record misses and disagreements too. This is a sharper test than asking whether the output looks polished.

## Pain signals: what is credible

**Moderate signal for recurring work that is hard to structure.** Across first-party READMEs, authors repeatedly package work around ambiguous requirements, plans, reviews, team health, retrospectives, and manager preparation. This supports the modest inference that people see reusable structure as useful enough to publish. It does not establish frequency or severity of the underlying pain. ([Matt Pocock catalog](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/README.md), [Han catalog](https://github.com/testdouble/han), [humantorch catalog](https://github.com/humantorch/skills-to-pay-the-bills), [bettersense catalog](https://github.com/shwetank/bettersense))

**Weak signal for this portfolio's precise need.** The adjacent projects do not establish unmet demand for this five-task decision-support bundle or demonstrate its benefit to frontline engineering contributors. Search-visible repositories are also not a representative sample of engineering teams. GitHub discovery skews toward people comfortable publishing agent tooling.

**No external efficacy evidence found in the inspected materials.** The reviewed project pages primarily document intended inputs, prompts, integration, outputs, and installation. They do not publish controlled comparisons or independently assessed organizational outcomes. A well-written skill file is a design artifact, not an efficacy result.

## Differentiation and overlap

The project's product frame is a compact set of **five engineering analysis and decision-support workflows** for users close to the work. The shared evidence contract—keeping user-provided facts, sourced facts, measurements, estimates, inferences, assumptions, and unknowns distinct—supports those workflows but is not a proven moat by itself. The close competitor also grounds its advice in an author's writing and experience, so personal provenance alone is not unique. These are design properties and a value hypothesis, not evidence of user preference or efficacy. ([Local README](../../../README.md), [shared decision/review contract](../../../agent/decision-and-review-contract.md), [manager-dot-dev README](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills))

Overlap is highest in the following areas:

- Planning and plan review overlap with Matt's specs/tickets and Han's feature/implementation planning and iterative plan review. Keep the local planning skill centered on investment choices, capacity, and deferral decisions rather than general task decomposition. ([Matt README](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/README.md), [Han planning docs](https://github.com/testdouble/han/tree/main/han-planning))
- Retrospectives and team-health summaries overlap with management-oriented collections and data-driven manager briefings. Differentiation requires system-level analysis grounded in provided evidence, explicit causal uncertainty, and actions with a way to check them—not another generic retro template. ([bettersense](https://github.com/shwetank/bettersense), [launganik](https://github.com/launganik/engineering-manager-skills))
- Reporting overlaps with stakeholder summaries and manager journals. The local workflow is strongest when it preserves evidence and contribution boundaries while shaping a specific decision for a reader. ([Han stakeholder summary](https://github.com/testdouble/han/blob/main/han-reporting/docs/skills/stakeholder-summary.md), [local portfolio review](../methodology/skills-portfolio-review.md))
- Metrics may be the clearest distinctive wedge because the inspected comparators emphasize work orchestration, management routines, or activity aggregation; the local skill explicitly starts by checking whether the measurement can support the requested decision. This is a comparison-based hypothesis, not a finding that no other skills do this.

## Utility evidence and current limits

The local repository has more behavior evidence than the inspected comparators expose publicly: eval prompts, rubric-scored examples, and some matched baseline/guided outputs. The portfolio review reports four paired cases for planning, review, reporting, and metric decision. It found one scored reporting actionability difference; other samples were already handled well by the baseline model. The management-retro record reports three paired cases, and explicitly limits the result to single-run qualitative judgments. This supports that the skills can impose a repeatable format and surface explicit fields in selected prompts. It does not yet show repeatable gains across users, models, languages, or real decisions. ([Portfolio review](../methodology/skills-portfolio-review.md), [management-retro evaluation](../methodology/management-retro-evaluation.md), [public-case evaluation](../methodology/public-case-evaluation.md))

No reviewed external repository provides a public, comparable evaluation that demonstrates the local portfolio is better or worse. Absence of published evidence is not evidence that a project has no utility; it means efficacy cannot be established from the inspected repository material.

## Risks and gaps

1. **Positioning breadth:** even five bounded workflows can read as a generic management toolkit. The README now leads with frontline engineering analysis and decision support, but this framing still needs user comprehension and adoption checks.
2. **Target-user assumption:** frontline engineers, tech leads, and product/data collaborators are the intended audience; inspected GitHub sources do not establish their demand or usage patterns for this bundle.
3. **Confident but unsupported judgment:** planning, retrospective, and executive communication can turn missing context into polished assumptions. The evidence contract is a meaningful safeguard, but evals must keep testing attribution, causality, unconfirmed ownership, and unsupported metrics.
4. **Weak adoption interpretation:** stars and forks are visible but confounded by creator reach, launch timing, novelty, and repository age. Downloads, if later available, would still not establish sustained use or outcomes.
5. **Agent compatibility:** local README says discovery/copy installation has been smoke-checked on Codex, while other agents have not been individually verified. Cross-agent compatibility claims should remain bounded to tested environments. ([README](../../../README.md))
6. **Privacy and integration:** management cases may contain sensitive team, personnel, metric, or business information. Adjacent systems that read Slack, calendars, Jira, and individual activity demonstrate a different integration surface and its data assumptions; the local file-based skills should specify what evidence the user supplies and avoid implying hidden access. ([launganik README](https://github.com/launganik/engineering-manager-skills), [humantorch README](https://github.com/humantorch/skills-to-pay-the-bills))

## Recommended validation wedge

Test `$metric-decision` with a narrow cohort of engineering/product decision makers and real, de-identified cases in which a metric moved near an instrumentation change, definition change, or cohort shift.

For each case, preserve the ordinary analysis as a baseline. Have the decision owner and a reviewer who understands the data assess both outputs against pre-agreed questions: Did it identify a real comparability or coverage problem? Did it distinguish observation from causal hypothesis? Did it change the action or confidence? Was any important caveat missed? How much review time did it take? Include cases where the baseline already succeeds and where the skill adds no value. Do not treat satisfaction with phrasing as outcome improvement.

Proceed only if the workflow repeatedly catches material issues or improves decision readiness without adding unsupported claims or disproportionate review work. If it mainly standardizes presentation, position it as a structured checklist and writing aid. This validation proposal is an inference from the local skill's explicit measurement-validity boundary and the adjacent repos' lack of published efficacy evidence.

### Design patterns worth borrowing without adding new capabilities

- **Use narrow triggers and explicit exclusions.** These files name concrete prompts and route adjacent cases elsewhere—for example, `business-literacy` sends engineering-metric-to-business questions to `developer-productivity`, while `delegation` sends prioritization to `roadmap-planning`. The local README and skill entrypoints already define routing boundaries; this pattern can sharpen those descriptions without creating a universal manager router. ([`business-literacy`](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills/blob/main/skills/business-literacy/SKILL.md), [`delegation`](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills/blob/main/skills/delegation/SKILL.md), [local README](../../../README.md))
- **Answer briefly before unfolding a framework.** The reviewed files direct the agent to state a recommendation, ask only questions that change the advice, and offer more depth when useful. This is a useful presentation choice for the local skills; it does not require importing the source project's opinionated frameworks. ([`roadmap-planning`](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills/blob/main/skills/roadmap-planning/SKILL.md), [`developer-productivity`](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills/blob/main/skills/developer-productivity/SKILL.md))
- **Give the user an operational next move.** Audience-ready wording, next action, caveat, owner/decision needed, and a verification signal make advice usable. Local skills already require evidence and action; keep these fields conditional on task and known facts rather than forcing every field into every answer. ([`written-communication`](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills/blob/main/skills/written-communication/SKILL.md), [`team-health`](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills/blob/main/skills/team-health/SKILL.md), [`delegation`](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills/blob/main/skills/delegation/SKILL.md))
- **Keep context updates conservative.** `em-context` explicitly excludes transient frustration and unvalidated speculation from persistent context. The local evidence-state rules can adopt this principle conceptually, but the local project should not claim shared memory or auto-update behavior unless that capability is separately designed and tested. ([`em-context`](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills/blob/main/skills/em-context/SKILL.md), [local evidence contract](../../../agent/decision-and-review-contract.md))

### What copying the bundle would expand

Copying these skills wholesale would move the local portfolio from five bounded decision workflows into a broad engineering-manager assistant: persistent team and employee profiles, individualized 1:1/feedback/performance/hiring/career support, team morale and burnout interventions, productivity metric programs, delegation practice, finance education, and general manager-message drafting. The upstream README explicitly describes those areas and shared context files. This would add sensitive personnel context and ongoing memory behavior, create more routing conflicts, broaden the trust claims, and dilute the current evidence-based planning/review/measurement/retrospective/reporting focus. ([`manager-skills` README](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills), [`em-context`](https://github.com/manager-dot-dev/manager-skills/blob/main/skills/em-context/SKILL.md))

**Assessment:** `manager-dot-dev/manager-skills` is a closer audience comparator than coding-agent packs, and it shows that roadmap tradeoffs, productivity measurement, business translation, written updates, team health, and delegation are already packaged as EM-specific agent skills. This increases overlap risk for broad labels such as “engineering management skills.” The local case is strongest when it stays more evidence-governed and decision-bounded: investment allocation, measurement validity, review of a concrete proposal, analysis of completed work, and evidence-preserving communication. The reusable lessons are narrow triggers, context-aware concise answers, and concrete next steps; importing the upstream breadth or context-memory architecture would change the product's scope.
